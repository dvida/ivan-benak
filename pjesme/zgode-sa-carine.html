Tales from the Customs Post

Devedeset nekog ljetau poratno ono doba,švercala se iz Mađarske,u Hrvatsku razna roba.

One year in the nineteen-nineties,in those days just after war,goods from Hungary to Croatiawent by smuggling, by the score.

Išli ljudi autobusom,nosila se svakog danarazna roba i odjeća,a najviše dobra hrana.

People travelled there by bus;every day, back home they'd bringgoods of every kind, and clothing,but good food above everything.

Bilo ljudi svih staleža.Jeftina je Mađa bila.Ali onda jednog dana,ovakva se zgoda zbila.

People came of every station,Hungary was cheap, you see.But then, one day, something happened,and here's how it came to be.

Jedna žena nekom popu,salame je dala dvije,pa ga lijepo zamolila,pod mantiju da sakrije.

To a priest a certain womanhanded two salamis, thenasked him nicely if he'd hide themunderneath his cassock's hem.

I carinik priđe popu,njegov glas se čuo jasni:"Šta imate za carinu,recite mi Velečasni?"

Then the customs man came over,and his voice rang loud and clear:"Tell me, Reverend, what have youto declare for customs here?"

"Nemam ništa, vjerujte mi,duša Bogu, tijelo meni,a to što imam međ' nogama,to pripada ovoj ženi."

"Nothing, trust me, I've got nothing:soul to God, and flesh to me,and what's there between my legs,that's this woman's property."

Tad carinik reče popu,videć' ženu lijepu, dragu:"Pripazite Velečasni,da ne ode duša k Vragu!"

Said the customs man to the priest,seeing her so sweet and fair:"Reverend, you'd best take care,or your soul's the Devil's share!"

Valpovo, 02. 06. 2005.

Valpovo, June 2, 2005

Translator's note. In the years after the 1991–95 war many Croatians crossed into Hungary by bus to buy cheap food and goods, and hid what they could from the customs officers on the way home.
