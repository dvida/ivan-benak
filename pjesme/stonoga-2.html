Poems for children

The Centipede

from the books Bunny's Luck (1990) and Through Life in Verse (2010)

Da obuje stonogasvoje noge male,došla u trgovinukupiti sandale.

To put shoes on allher little feet, one daythe centipede went to the shopfor sandals, so they say.

Trgovac je vidioda je šansa mala –gdje da nađe tolikiisti broj sandala?

The shopkeeper could seethe chances were quite small —where could he find so manysandals in one size at all?

Jedva da su pronašlidvadesetak pari,a tek su je obulipetinu, u stvari.

Scarcely did they findsome twenty pairs in store,and that had shod a fifthof her feet, and no more.

Pa su zatim cipeleobuvati stali,ali je i u tomeizbor bio mali.

And so they started tryingshoes on her instead,but in shoes, as well, the choicewas small, it must be said.

Probali su nanule,čizme i opanke,da sve noge obujunade su pretanke.

They tried on wooden clogs,boots, opanci too;of shoeing all her feetthe hopes were thin and few.

Na kraju je ostaloosam bosih noga,ljuta kao paprikabila je stonoga.

In the end, eight feetwere bare still, and indeed,as fiery as a chilliwas the centipede.

Zamislite kako jeizgledala radnja,sve dok je isprobanai patika zadnja.

Just imagine howthe shop looked — what a mess! —before they'd tried the verylast sneaker on, no less.

Trgovac se trudioobuti stonogu,na kraju od umoraon je pao s nogu.

The shopkeeper worked hardto shoe the centipede;at last, from sheer exhaustion,he was run off his feet.

Od silnoga neredadigla mu se kosa,stonoga je otišlaiz dućana bosa.

From all the dreadful messup on end stood his hair,and the centipede walked outof the shop with her feet all bare.

Eto, zato stonogeobuću ne nose,nego brižno čuvajusvoje noge bose.

So that's the reason whycentipedes wear no shoes,but take good care insteadof the bare feet they use.

Translator's note. Opanci are the traditional leather peasant shoes of the Balkans. "Ljut" means both hot and angry, so the chilli is a fiery temper. "Pao s nogu", literally "fell off his feet", means dead tired, and English "run off his feet" keeps the joke.
