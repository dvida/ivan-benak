Radio burza

Rezultat trgovine

iz knjige Stihom kroz život (2010.)

Radio market

The Results of My Trading

from the book Through Life in Verse (2010)

Ovaj puta ništa ne prodajem,već rezultat trgovine dajem.

This time I'm selling nothing, not at all;I'll give you my trade results, great and small.

Oglase sam preko Burze dao,mnoge stvari tako sam prodao.

I placed my ads upon the Burza, andsold many things that way, you understand.

U početku, bilo je to prije,peć sam prod'o što ugodno grije.

At first — this was a good while back, before —I sold a stove that warms you to the core.

Jednom momku, bio je bez para,dadoh gume za steku cigara.

One lad was broke, he had no cash that day —for a carton of cigarettes, the tyres went his way.

U krumpiru imao sam sreću,za pedeset kuna dadoh vreću.

In spuds I had some luck, I have to say:at fifty kunas a sack they went away.

Piliće je uzela šnaš' Kata,imala je za njih kvočku tada.

The chicks were taken by Missus Kata; shehad a broody hen to raise them, you see.

Mijenjah kupus kod gospođe Mice,za šunkicu njene male gice.

I traded cabbage with Mrs Mica, andher little pig's small ham came to my hand.

Kozliće sam sa kozom predao,dobra koza, pa mi za njom žao.

The kids went off together with the goat —a good goat, and I miss her, I must note.

Gospi Mari drva sam odnio,sve u njenu šupicu složio.

To Mrs Mara I took the firewood round,and stacked it in her little shed, safe and sound.

Žensku bundu vrjedniju od zlata,za rođendan kupi ženi Mata.

Mata bought his wife, for her birthday,a lady's fur coat, worth more than gold, I'd say.

Ode sijeno nije ga ni dosta,kukuruza tek tri vreće osta.

The hay has gone — there wasn't even enough;of maize, three sacks are all that's left of the stuff.

Za kolinje komplet sam prodao,a rakijski kazan mi ostao.

I sold the kolinje kit, the lot; but stillI'm left with my rakija-making still.

Otišla je šljivovica moja,ostala je tek litrica koja.

My slivovitz has gone as well, and see,there's just a litre left, or so, for me.

Orahe sam prodao do juče',sada nemam za potrebe kuće.

I sold my walnuts, right up to yesterday;for my own house there's none now, I must say.

A kamin sam prodao još prije,kumu Jozi, da mu kuću grije.

And earlier the fireplace was soldto Kum Jozo, to warm his house from cold.

Plamenik je o'š'o, Bogu hvala,pokraj Drave kuća je ostala.

The burner went, thank God, it's off my hands;the little house beside the Drava stands.

Sudoper je kupila snaš' Manda,u salašu pere suđe sada.

The sink went off to Missus Manda's place:out at her farmstead now she washes plates.

Krevet snova jučer je kupio,jedan mladić, tek se oženio.

A bed of dreams was bought just yesterdayby a young man who'd just got wed, they say.

Za mast kante kupio je Ante,a kacigu moj sinovac Mate.

The cans for lard were bought by Ante, straight;the helmet — that went to my nephew Mate.

Stevo kupi ženi Rozi poni,pa se selom sad voza i zvoni.

Stevo bought his wife Roza a pony, sonow round the village she drives and rings as she goes.

Za rakiju kupi Dragan bure,a za vino iz Belišća Jure.

A barrel for rakija went to Dragan, sure,and one for wine to Belišće's own Jure.

Zamrzivač kupio je Zrinko,televizor iz Baranje Mirko.

The freezer was bought by Zrinko, and the TVby Mirko, who's from Baranja, you see.

Novog gazdu sad štenci imaju,još dva imam, neka ljudi znaju.

The puppies have a brand-new master, andI've two left still — so let folk understand.

I ta dva bih ja nekome dao,samo kad bih dobre ljude znao.

And those two I would gladly give away,if I knew some good people — so I say.

Eto, to su moji rezultatii dalje ću ja oglase dati.

Well, there — those are the results I've got to show,and I'll keep placing ads, and on I'll go.

Nek' se dalje kolo Burze kreće,svima puno trgovačke sreće.

So may the Burza's kolo keep turning round,and trading luck for all of you abound.

Nek' vam bude veselo i živo,to vam želi iz Valpova Ivo.

May all your days be merry, lively, too —this, Ivo from Valpovo wishes you.

Valpovo, 20. 10. 2006.

Valpovo, October 20, 2006

Translator's note. A humorous report to Radio burza listeners on what his earlier verse ads sold, and to whom. "Missus" stands for šnaša, a folk form of address for a married woman; a kum is a godfather or wedding sponsor, a lifelong family tie. The "kolo" of the Burza in the second-last couplet is both its wheel and a circle dance.
