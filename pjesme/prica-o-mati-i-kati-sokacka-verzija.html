The Tale of Mata and Kata (Šokac Version)

U oskudno ovo vrime,Mata Katu oženio.U domaji posla nima,težak im je život bio.

In these hard and hungry daysMata took his Kata to wed.Work at home was nowhere found,and a heavy life they led.

U pečalbu ode Mata,otvorio zapad vrata.Ali doma osta plačuć',ucviljena lipa Kata.

Mata left to work abroad,for the West had opened its door;and at home, in tears, stayed Kata,lovely Kata, grieving sore.

Na baušteli kod Jericha,Mata se je zaposlio,pisma, marke doma slaoi na Katu je mislio.

At the Jericha building siteMata found some work to do;letters, marks he sent back home,and he thought of Kata too.

Kratko vrime nakon toga,prvog sina rodi Kata.Blistala je sva od sriće,jer sritan će bit i tata.

Just a little time thereafterKata bore their first-born boy;all aglow she was with gladness,for the dad would share the joy.

Al' samoća dušu truje,mladost traži želje svoje,a noći su puste, duge,lipši život je u dvoje.

Loneliness will poison souls,youth has longings of its own,and the nights are long and empty:life is sweeter not alone.

A Kata je mlada bila,imala je burne snove,tilu ljubav priuštilai rađala sokolove.

Kata, after all, was young,and she dreamed such stormy dreams;to her body love she granted,and bore falcon sons, it seems.

I prođoše deset lita,doma Mata odlučio,na baušteli u Švabijiinvalidsku zaradio.

Ten years passed, and Mata settledthat he'd go back home to stay:on a site in Švabijahe'd earned disability pay.

Mislio je da će s Katom,sriću, radost podiliti,lipu kuću sagraditi,mnogo dice izroditi.

He had thought that he and Katajoy and happiness would share,build themselves a lovely house,and have many children there.

Al' sudba je drugo tila.I Kata je vridna bila.Dok je Mata arbajtovo,dica su kuću popunila.

But fate willed another way,Kata, too, worked hard and well:while Mata was off arbeiting,children filled the house, truth to tell.

Pred kućerak stiže Mata,izletila pred njeg' Kata.Lipo dicu odgojila.Dica viču: "Ide tata!"

Mata reached the little house,Kata flew out to his side;she had raised the children nicely."Here comes Daddy!" children cried.

"Čija su to dica Kato?Reci meni, ženo mila,u Njemačku kad sam oš'o,kuća nam je prazna bila."

"Whose are all these children, Kata?Tell me, wife, my dearest one:when I left for Germany,in this house there wasn't one."

Umiljato poče Kata:"Ovi dragi sokolovi,viruj Mata svojoj Kati,sve su naši to sinovi."

Sweetly Kata then began:"These dear falcons, one by one —Mata, trust your Kata here —every one of them's our son."

"Reći ću ti sve po redu:Kad si oš'o u Švabiju,kratko vrime nakon toga,rodila sam tvog Iliju."

"I will tell you all in order:when you'd gone to Švabija,just a little while thereafterI gave birth to your Ilija."

"A onda sam jedne noći,u snovima s tobom bila,ostala sam trudna, Mata,sina Peru sam rodila."

"Then one night I was with you,in my dreams, as it befell;I was left with child, dear Mata,and gave birth to Pero as well."

"Često sam te poželilanajgore su noći bilei kad nisam mogla durat,kao s neba pade Mile."

"Often I would long for you,nights were hardest, I confess,and when I could bear no more,Mile dropped from heaven, no less."

"Sićaš li se, dragi Mata,kad mi marke nisi slao?Mislila sam samo na te,Bog mi tada Đuku dao."

"Mata dear, do you rememberwhen the marks stopped coming through?I kept thinking just of you,and God gave me Đuka, too."

Sluša Mata mile riči,kako Kata sve razlaže.Pa kad skonta to u glavi,misli, možda i ne laže.

Mata hears her tender words,how she sets it out, and why;and when he has worked it through,thinks: perhaps she doesn't lie.

"Ih, još kada se moji rode,bit će dice kuća puna.Bar sa dicom ja ću biti,bogatiji od tajkuna."

"Ha! And once my own are born,there'll be children in every room;then at least in kids I'll bericher than any tycoon."

Valpovo, 16. 08. 2004.

Valpovo, August 16, 2004

Translator's note. A retelling in Šokac dialect of his comic tale of a guest worker. Švabija ("Swabia") is the folk name for Germany, "arbeiting" echoes the German words the guest workers brought home, and "falcons" is the folk word for fine sons.
