Umirovljenički jadi

Pensioners' woes

A Pensioner's Woes

from the book Through Life in Verse (2010)

Mirovino moja, teško zarađenai pod stare dane otuđena.Blagodati svoga rada snivam,petnaestoga crkavicu primam.Za tu Vladu starci su glasali,da bi bolji standard uživali.Kažem javno, neka je na znanje,obećanje ludom radovanje.Kome da se ja žalim i tužim,osam dana penzijom se služim.Gordo idem sa hrpom računa.da odigram ulogu tajkuna.Sve polako od vrata do vrata,a na kraju groznica me hvata.Cijene rastu, sve to više skaču,treba živjet', oh Bože, kako ću?Sa svih strana računi me tište.plaćam vodu i gradsko zemljište.Još u mjesnu zajednicu svratim,komunalnu naknadu da platim.Pa telefon i TV dok platim,prijatelju pozajmicu vratim.I dopunsko još osiguranje,u kasi je novaca sve manje.Plaćam struju, odvoz gradskog smeća,bar da mi je mirovina veća!Plinara mi opomenu šalje,vjerujte mi, tako neće dalje.Dok podmirim svima članarinu,pa i Crkvi platim crkvarinu.I Hrvatske kad podmirim vode,zadnja kuna iz kase mi ode.Sve to plaćam, nikoga ne krivim,al' recite, od čega da živim?Cijene rastu ne tiče se Vlade,a Boga mi, ni za naše jade.Tugo moja, svatko svoje traži,za nas starce tu zakon ne važi.Bogu božje i vrag traži svoje,nema više mirovine moje.Kad pogledam što u kasi osta,taman, brale, za rakiju dosta.Da popijem i ublažim tugučekajući crkavicu drugu.

My pension, O my pension, hard-earned pay,in my old age from me now snatched away.I dream the blessings of my work would come;the fifteenth brings a pittance, a mere crumb.The old folk voted for this Governmentto have a better standard — that's what was meant.I say it publicly, so all may hear:a promise only makes the foolish cheer.To whom should I complain, to whom lament?In eight days' time my pension is all spent.With pride I set off, bills stacked in a pile,and play the part of a tycoon a while.I slowly go from one door to the next,and at the end I'm feverish and vexed.The prices rise and jump up more and more;I have to live — oh God, how, I implore?From every side the bills weigh down on me:I pay for water, and the town land fee.Then to the local office I must goto pay the council's service charge, you know.Then phone and TV licence, and I mustrepay the loan a friend gave me in trust.Then top-up health insurance must be paid,and less and less is left of what I've made.I pay for power, for the rubbish haul —oh, if my pension only weren't so small!The gas works sends a warning note my way;believe me, this can't go on another day.And then my membership fees, every one,and to the Church its church dues, when that's done.And when I've paid off Croatian Waters too,and my last kuna bids my purse adieu.I pay it all, I don't blame anyone,but tell me, what am I to live upon?The prices rise — the Government won't care,and, God knows, nor for all the woes we bear.Oh, sorrow mine! Each fights for his own share;for us old folk the law just isn't there.To God what's God's, the devil wants his too —and of my pension nothing's left, it's true.And when I see what's left inside my till,there's just enough, old mate, for rakija still,to drink it down and ease my grief a bit,and wait for the next pittance after it.

Valpovo, 19. 07. 2000.

Valpovo, July 19, 2000

Translator's note. "A promise makes a fool glad" is a Croatian proverb. The bills are real ones: the municipal land and services charges, Croatian Waters (the state water-management levy) and the church contribution. The kuna was Croatia's currency until 2023.
